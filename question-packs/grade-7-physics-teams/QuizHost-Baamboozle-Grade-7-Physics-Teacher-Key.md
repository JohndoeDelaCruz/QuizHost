# Grade 7 Physics Team Challenge — Teacher Key

24 original multiple-choice questions for QuizHost's Baamboozle-style shared-screen team mode. This is classroom discussion and reasoning practice.

## Import and host

1. Open **Quiz library → Import** and select **QuizHost-Baamboozle-Grade-7-Physics.json**.
2. Import once: the file adds three versions of the same question bank. Choose the version that matches your number of teams below.
3. Select **Host game → Better together**, enter the team names, and open the room.
4. Start, let the active team choose an unused tile, and discuss the question. Record its choice and reasoning before reveal or timeout. Reveal the answer, choose **Correct** or **Missed it**, then continue after the rankings.
5. No EXE rebuild, internet connection or separate student app is needed. These questions use text and numerical data, with no external images.

## Discussion and scoring

- Each question allows 60–90 seconds of answering/discussion, after QuizHost's preparation and reading stages.
- Suggested rule: award the tile's 100 points only when the team gives the correct choice and explains the central idea shown in the key. Accept equivalent reasoning in English or Filipino; exact wording is not required. Announce the rule before play.
- **Correct** awards the full tile value; **Missed it** awards zero. The current game does not automatically grade or save oral explanations. A teacher can note the reasoning separately.
- The follow-up prompts below are optional. Ask them after deciding the tile result so extra discussion does not change one team's scoring rule.
- Re-importing adds another copy of each quiz. Keep this answer key teacher-only.

## Explanations and optional follow-up prompts

### Master question 1: Forces

During a school tug-of-war, one side pulls a rope 240 N east and the other pulls it 180 N west. Consider these two horizontal forces only. Which statement is correct?

**Answer C:** The net force is 60 N east, so the horizontal forces are unbalanced.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Opposite forces subtract: 240 - 180 = 60 N, in the direction of the larger force. Net force predicts a change in velocity; it does not by itself give the rope's current speed.

**Optional follow-up:** If the westward pull increases to 240 N, what happens to the net force?

### Master question 2: Forces

A student pushes a toy cart 12 N to the right. It moves in a straight line at constant speed. The only horizontal forces are the push and friction. What can the team infer about friction?

**Answer A:** Friction is 12 N to the left, balancing the push.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Straight-line constant speed means constant velocity and zero net force. The opposing horizontal force must equal the 12 N push.

**Optional follow-up:** If the push briefly increases while friction stays the same, how would the cart's motion change?

### Master question 3: Forces

A book rests on a classroom table. Carlo says, 'No forces act on the book because it is not moving.' Which response best explains the situation?

**Answer D:** Gravity pulls down and the table pushes up; these forces balance on the book.

**Reasoning to listen for:** The book has an upward support force and a downward gravitational force. Being at rest here means the net force is zero, not that each force is absent.

**Optional follow-up:** What would change if the table no longer supported the book?

### Master question 4: Forces

A cart is already moving left in a straight line. All forces on it remain balanced. Which prediction follows from this information?

**Answer B:** It continues left at the same speed while the forces remain balanced.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Balanced forces do not change velocity. They can describe an object at rest or an object already moving with constant velocity.

**Optional follow-up:** Why is zero net force different from zero velocity?

### Master question 5: Forces

A team wants to compare how far the same toy car rolls on cloth and smooth board. Which plan gives the fairest comparison?

**Answer A:** Release the same car from the same ramp height, change only the surface, and repeat the trials.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Keep the car, release height and procedure the same while changing the surface. Repeated trials reduce the influence of an unusual run.

**Optional follow-up:** Which variable is changed, and what outcome is measured?

### Master question 6: Forces

Two groups pull a rope: 200 N east and 200 N west. Which change would create a net force toward the east?

**Answer C:** Increase the eastward pull by 50 N and keep the westward pull unchanged.

**Reasoning to listen for:** The eastward pull becomes 250 N while the westward pull remains 200 N. The resulting net force is 50 N east. Increasing both sides equally keeps them balanced.

**Optional follow-up:** Name a different change that would also produce a 50 N eastward net force.

### Master question 7: Forces

A toy car follows a curved track at constant speed. A teammate says the net force must be zero because the speed is constant. Which response is best?

**Answer B:** Its direction changes, so its velocity changes and the forces cannot all be balanced.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Velocity includes direction as well as speed. A curved path changes direction, so constant speed on that path does not mean constant velocity. Changing velocity requires a net force.

**Optional follow-up:** What extra information does velocity include that speed does not?

### Master question 8: Forces

The same cart is pulled at the same steady speed across two level surfaces. Required pulls are P: 0.8, 0.9, 0.8 N; Q: 1.7, 1.8, 1.6 N. The method is otherwise unchanged. What is best supported?

**Answer D:** Surface Q has greater opposing resistance under these test conditions.

**Reasoning to listen for:** At steady speed, the pull balances opposing resistance. Every Q reading is higher than every P reading, supporting greater resistance for Q in this controlled comparison.

**Optional follow-up:** Why do repeated measurements strengthen the comparison?

### Master question 9: Motion

From a barangay hall, Nika walks 60 m east, then 20 m west along the same straight road. What are her total distance and final displacement from the hall?

**Answer B:** Distance: 80 m; displacement: 40 m east.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Distance adds the full path: 60 + 20 = 80 m. Displacement compares final and initial positions: 60 - 20 = 40 m east.

**Optional follow-up:** Where would Nika need to finish for her displacement to be zero?

### Master question 10: Motion

A pupil walks 120 m in 40 s, then waits for 20 s. What is the average speed for the entire 60 s observation, including the wait?

**Answer D:** 2 m/s, because 120 m is divided by the full 60 s.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Average speed uses total distance divided by the entire specified elapsed time. Here, 120 / 60 = 2 m/s. The speed during the walking part alone is 3 m/s.

**Optional follow-up:** How can the walking speed and the average speed over the whole observation both be correct?

### Master question 11: Motion

A cart travels straight away from its start. Its distances are 0 m at 0 s, 4 m at 2 s, 8 m at 4 s, and 12 m at 6 s. If the pattern continues, how far is it from the start at 10 s?

**Answer A:** 20 m: it gains 4 m every 2 s.

**Reasoning to listen for:** The data show 2 m/s. From 6 s to 10 s there are two more 2 s intervals, adding 8 m to 12 m.

**Optional follow-up:** What assumption makes this prediction reasonable?

### Master question 12: Motion

A cart moves straight forward. Total distances are 0 m at 0 s, 2 m at 2 s, 6 m at 4 s, and 12 m at 6 s. What do the distances covered during each 2 s interval suggest?

**Answer C:** Its average speed increases across the intervals: it covers 2 m, then 4 m, then 6 m.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Compare the additional distance in equal time intervals, not just the total distance. Increasing interval distances mean increasing interval-average speeds.

**Optional follow-up:** Why is 'the total distance increases' alone not enough to show that speed increases?

### Master question 13: Motion

Team Sampaguita covers 60 m in 20 s. Team Narra covers 100 m in 40 s. Narra claims it was faster because it travelled farther. Which conclusion is supported?

**Answer D:** Sampaguita has the greater average speed: 3 m/s compared with 2.5 m/s.

**Reasoning to listen for:** A fair comparison uses distance per unit time. Sampaguita: 60 / 20 = 3 m/s; Narra: 100 / 40 = 2.5 m/s.

**Optional follow-up:** What extra information is needed when someone reports only a distance?

### Master question 14: Motion

Two pupils walk along the same straight path at 3 m/s, one east and one west. Which statement correctly compares their motion?

**Answer B:** Their speeds are equal, but their velocities differ because their directions differ.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Speed gives magnitude only. Velocity includes direction, so 3 m/s east and 3 m/s west are different velocities.

**Optional follow-up:** What would need to match for both pupils to have the same velocity?

### Master question 15: Motion

On a position-time graph for a cart on a straight track, a horizontal line stays at 5 m from 10 s to 30 s. What does this part of the graph mean?

**Answer C:** The cart stays at the same position while time passes, so it is at rest during that interval.

**Reasoning to listen for:** The vertical coordinate is unchanged throughout the continuous graph segment. A flat position-time segment represents zero velocity over that interval.

**Optional follow-up:** How would the graph look if the cart moved steadily farther from the reference point?

### Master question 16: Motion

Using the classroom door as the reference point, a pupil starts 5 m west of it and finishes 5 m east of it 20 s later. What is the average velocity over that time?

**Answer A:** 0.5 m/s east, because the displacement is 10 m east over 20 s.

**Reasoning to listen for:** From 5 m west to 5 m east is a net change of 10 m east. Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time: 10 / 20 = 0.5 m/s east.

**Optional follow-up:** Why do we need both the starting and finishing positions?

### Master question 17: Heat

One end of a metal spoon sits in warm soup. After a while, the handle also becomes warmer although it does not touch the soup. Which explanation is best?

**Answer C:** Energy transfers through the metal from its warmer end toward its cooler end by conduction.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Conduction transfers energy through interactions within the metal. The spoon does not have to flow as a liquid or touch the soup along its full length.

**Optional follow-up:** Why might a wooden handle warm more slowly under comparable conditions?

### Master question 18: Heat

A metal ruler and a wooden ruler have both been measured at 25 degrees C. Your hand is warmer than both. The metal feels cooler. What is the best explanation?

**Answer A:** Metal transfers energy away from your warmer hand faster, even though the rulers have the same temperature.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Touch depends partly on the rate of energy transfer, not temperature alone. Metal conducts energy away from the warmer hand more quickly than wood.

**Optional follow-up:** Which is stronger evidence of the rulers' temperatures: touch alone or a suitable thermometer? Why?

### Master question 19: Heat

Water is heated from below in a container. Warmer water rises and cooler water sinks. Which explanation best accounts for the circulation?

**Answer B:** Heating changes the water's density, and the bulk movement transfers energy by convection.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Warmer liquid water is generally less dense over the ordinary warm-water range and rises as denser, cooler water sinks. This circulation carries energy through the liquid.

**Optional follow-up:** Why can a solid spoon not circulate in the same way?

### Master question 20: Heat

Energy from the Sun crosses the nearly empty space between the Sun and Earth. Which process explains how that energy reaches Earth?

**Answer D:** Radiation, which can transfer energy through empty space.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Radiation does not require matter between the source and receiver. Conduction and convection require material to carry energy.

**Optional follow-up:** Why would the absence of air not prevent sunlight from warming a surface?

### Master question 21: Heat

Identical cups each start with 20 g of ice under the same room conditions. One is wrapped in felt. After 20 min, 8 g of ice remains in the wrapped cup and 3 g in the unwrapped cup. What is best supported?

**Answer A:** The felt slows energy transfer into the ice, so less ice melts during the test.

**Reasoning to listen for:** More ice remains in the insulated cup, consistent with slower energy transfer from the warmer surroundings. Insulation reduces transfer; it does not create cold or make transfer impossible.

**Optional follow-up:** What starting conditions must be the same for this comparison to be useful?

### Master question 22: Heat

To test whether a lid slows cooling, one group puts 100 mL of water at 70 degrees C in a covered cup and 200 mL at 60 degrees C in an uncovered cup. Why is the comparison weak?

**Answer D:** The water volume and starting temperature also differ, so the lid's effect is not isolated.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Besides the lid, the group changed water volume and starting temperature. A better comparison uses identical cups, equal water volumes and equal initial temperatures, then changes the lid condition only.

**Optional follow-up:** Redesign the test so the lid is the only planned difference.

### Master question 23: Heat

One cup contains 50 mL of water and another contains 200 mL. Both start at 60 degrees C. Both will cool to 30 degrees C without evaporation. Which statement is correct?

**Answer C:** They start at the same temperature, but more energy must leave the 200 mL sample for the same temperature drop.

**Reasoning to listen for:** Temperature alone does not specify the energy transferred. For the same substance and temperature change, the larger mass of water releases more energy. Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.

**Optional follow-up:** Why is 'more water' different from 'higher temperature'?

### Master question 24: Heat

Identical model roofs, painted black or white, start at the same temperature and receive sunlight side by side. Three equal-duration tests give roof temperatures: black 41, 42, 43 degrees C; white 34, 35, 36 degrees C. Which decision is best supported?

**Answer B:** Choose white to reduce roof-surface temperature under conditions like these tests; verify other conditions before generalizing.

**Reasoning to listen for:** The white model roof is cooler in every paired test. This supports the choice for similar conditions, while avoiding an unlimited claim about every material, location or weather condition.

**Optional follow-up:** What extra test would help before making a recommendation for an actual school building?
